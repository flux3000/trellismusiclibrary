Add Recordings — two redesigns

Both directions replace the always-expanded triage cards with something that survives a folder of hundreds — maybe thousands — of shows, per your brief. They differ in where the person's attention goes: A keeps every recording on screen, compressed into one scannable row, and adds bulk actions. B keeps almost nothing on screen — it auto-files what the scoring is already confident about and only ever shows you the ones it isn't.

What A fixes

  • One line per recording instead of a full metrics card, always. 214 shows fits on a screen you can actually scan; today's cards would run for pages.
  • Filter chips (All / High / Medium / Low) so "show me only what needs my judgment" — the Medium and Low tiers — is one click, not a scroll.
  • Bulk bar — select a tier, one button ingests all of it. This is the piece that makes 1,000 shows tractable: you triage the 5% that's ambiguous and mass-accept the rest.
  • Expand-in-place for the one row you want the full metric breakdown on, rather than it being forced on all 214 whether you asked or not.
  • The "In library" badge stays exactly where you already have it (the 8/26 fix); "New performer" doesn't show here either, same reasoning as the folder picker.

What B does differently

  • Most of the work never reaches you. At 1,842 folders, 92% auto-filing themselves is the difference between a task and a chore — you open the page to 12 decisions, not 1,842.
  • The stat band is the whole status report — found / auto-filed / needs you / already-owned — the four numbers you'd actually want to know before scrolling further.
  • One card, one decision. Each exception shows exactly why it stopped (the reason badge), the specific field that's uncertain, and three actions: accept, fix and open the full form, or reject.
  • The auto-filed log is a receipt, not a queue — collapsed by default, because if the scoring was right about 1,690 shows there's nothing to review there.
  • Cost: this needs the confidence threshold for "auto-file without asking" to be a real, tuned number — get it wrong and either good shows get quietly buried or bad ones get filed unattended.

My recommendation

B, with A's table as the "View all" screen underneath it. You told me the real case is hundreds or thousands at once — that's a scale where a human looking at every row, even one compressed line each, stops being the bottleneck-fix and starts being the bottleneck. B is the only one of the two that actually removes work rather than compressing it.

The risk is trust: the first time B auto-files something wrong unattended is the last time you trust it unattended. That's a real cost, not a hypothetical one — which is why I'd build A's table first regardless. It's the correct redesign of what exists today, ships faster, and B's "needs attention" queue is a filtered view of that exact same table once you're comfortable raising the auto-file threshold.

Practically: ship A. Add the auto-file threshold as an opt-in once you've watched A's High tier be right often enough to trust it unattended — B becomes almost a free feature at that point, not a second build.

Two things I need you to decide

  • What confidence threshold earns auto-file? B assumes "High" (today's scoring band) is enough to skip human review entirely. If that's too aggressive, B's whole value — 92% needing zero clicks — shrinks along with it. Is High good enough to trust unattended, or does that only apply to something stricter, like High and an exact performer match?
  • Does Reject in the queue delete the folder, or just skip it? Neither mockup answers this, and it's a real data question — a Low-confidence cassette rip might be genuinely worth keeping around to revisit later, not a mis-scan to discard.
