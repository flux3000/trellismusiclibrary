Add Recording: redesign options

The metadata review step is the last page in the app still running the pre-August information panel: a vertical tab rail, a header inside every pane repeating the name of the tab above it, its own quality bar, and the browser's native audio widget. View Recording moved past all four on 18 and 21 August. This sheet puts two directions side by side, then treats the preview player as its own decision, because it is the one piece with a real cost attached.

What A gets you

  • It is almost entirely deletion. Three pane headers, one quality bar, one vertical rail, one .ingest-tabs rule set. The horizontal component already exists and is already styled; extending it here is one class on the wrapper, exactly as the CSS comment on .slide-panel--htabs predicted.
  • Two tabs the page was missing. Quality and AI Assist. AI Assist is not new functionality, it is the button from the quality bar landing where its output already appears.
  • The panel becomes collapsible, which the drag handle only approximates. A reviewer typing titles from the info file wants it open. A reviewer fixing a date wants the full width for the track table.
  • Risk: low, and mostly cosmetic regression risk in the panes that reuse .rev-raw-section and .cksum-*, which today deliberately keep a denser padding than View Recording. Moving to the shared component means accepting the looser padding or scoping it.
  • What A does not fix: the page still opens as a wall of form fields. Nothing at the top tells you what record you are about to create.

Direction B: Record PreviewB

Everything in A, plus one structural move: the four fields that identify the show (performer, date, venue, event) lift out of the scrolling form into a header block drawn the way View Recording draws its recording header. The page then opens with the record as it will look once filed, and the rest of the form (location, source, lineage, quality, personnel, notes, official) drops into a Details section under the tracks. Same fields, same order within each group, different hierarchy: identity first, evidence second, everything else after that.

What B does differently

  • The page answers "what am I filing" before it asks you anything. Today the answer is a folder name in the topbar and eight inputs you have to read to reconstruct it. A show has an identity, and four of the fields are that identity while the other nine are attributes of it. The form does not currently make that distinction.
  • It is the same header the record will have. Add & View currently takes you to a page that looks nothing like the one you just filled in. In B the two are recognisably the same object, before and after.
  • "Inferred from" is new and is the interesting part. The scan already knows whether each field came from a FLAC tag, the info file, or the folder name, and the source detector now records source_from_folder_name explicitly. Today none of that reaches the screen, so a value read off a folder name looks exactly as authoritative as one read from a tag. That is worth more than the layout change.
  • Cost: real. The header is new CSS, not a reuse, and the promoted fields need their autocomplete dropdowns (performer, venue, event) re-anchored to header positions rather than form rows. Call it two to three times A.
  • Risk: the date field. Three number inputs read as a header line only if they are drawn as one control, and the current Year / Mo / Day triple is deliberately forgiving about partial dates. Whatever replaces it must stay as forgiving, or B quietly makes a common case harder.

The preview playerP

Today this is <audio id="ingest-preview-audio" controls>: a native widget the OS draws at its own weight, radius and palette. It is the only control in the app that does not use the design tokens, and on the packaged WKWebView build it looks like a piece of Safari sitting inside Trellis. It streams from /api/stream/ingest-preview, which stays the same in all three options below. Independent of A and B, pick one.

P1: Transport strip

03 Riddles 1:12 4:26

The player bar's own vocabulary at about two thirds scale: the same accent-filled circle for play and pause, the same Lucide skip glyphs, the same input[type=range].progress-bar with its accent gradient fill and 10px thumb, the same tabular-nums times. Prev and next step through the track table, which the native widget cannot do at all. No new dependencies, no new decode work, and the range input keeps keyboard seeking for free.

P2: Waveform

03 Riddles 1:12 / 4:26

This one has a measured cost. View Recording's waveform renders from peaks already stored in the database, computed during analysis. A pre-ingest folder has no analysis and no peaks, so wavesurfer would have to fetch and decode the file itself. On the show I measured (Bob Dylan, 2002-10-07, disc 1, 16 bit) the median track is 37 MB and the largest is 57 MB, and the whole disc is 445 MB. A 24 bit / 96 kHz show, which is what prompted this redesign, is several times that per track. Every preview click would pull and decode tens of megabytes before drawing anything, for a control whose job is "let me hear ten seconds of track 4 to check the title is right". The alternative is computing peaks server-side during triage, which is real compute added to the fast path we just spent a day making fast.

Recommendation

A now, plus P1, plus B's "Inferred from" block

Ship A. Six of the seven differences in the table at the top are the same decision you already made in August and it simply never got carried to this page. That is not a redesign, it is finishing one, and it is mostly deleting code: three pane headers, one quality bar, one vertical rail, one .ingest-tabs rule set. Reusing the component also means this page inherits the next change to it for free instead of drifting again.

Take P1 for the player. P2 looks the best of the three and is the one I would want if the peaks were free, but they are not free here and the numbers are not close: tens of megabytes fetched and decoded per click for a control used to confirm a track title. If you want a waveform on this page, the honest version is server-side peaks computed during triage, and that is a separate decision about triage speed, not a player decision.

Take B's "Inferred from" block regardless of whether you take B. It is the only genuinely new information on either mockup. The scan already knows the provenance of every field, and after this week's source work it knows when a value came from a folder name rather than a tag. Right now a guess and a fact are rendered identically. That is a data-integrity gap wearing a layout costume, and it fits in A's topbar or as a right-hand column of the form without B's header.

Hold B. The header is right about hierarchy and I would expect to build it eventually, but its cost is real (new CSS, three autocomplete dropdowns re-anchored, and a date control that has to stay as forgiving about partial dates as the current triple). It also reads better as a decision you make after living with A for a week, because A is a strict subset of B: nothing in A gets thrown away if you go on to build B.

Three things I need you to decide

  • Does the Quality tab belong here at all? View Recording has one because the recording has been analysed. On this page, in Quick Add mode, it has not been. The tab would show either the triage pass's partial numbers or an empty state saying analysis runs after filing. Both are defensible; an empty tab on a five-tab strip is not free.
  • Panel default: open or collapsed? The rail makes it a toggle, so this is only about the first paint. Open matches today and suits transcribing titles from an info file. Collapsed gives the track table the full width, which is what you want when the info file is empty, and that is common in the folders coming out of triage.
  • Pane padding. The shared component pads panes for a full-height reading surface. This page deliberately runs denser. Adopting the component means either accepting the looser padding here or keeping a scoped override, which is a small permanent divergence of exactly the kind this sheet is trying to remove.
